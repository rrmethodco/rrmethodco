# Brand Naming — 4-agent synthesis

Four naming agents ran in parallel: creative direction (content-marketer), sales lens (sales-automator), competitive landscape (search-specialist), strategic framework (startup-analyst). Full transcripts are in `~/.../tasks/*.output`. This is the synthesized recommendation.

## Strategic posture (from startup-analyst)

The Phase 3 TAM (~200K dental practices + DSOs) is 36× the Phase 1 TAM (~5,500 endo practices). The biggest naming mistake is picking an endo-coded name that wins the pilot 5% faster and forces a six-figure rebrand at exactly the moment we're raising a Series A and onboarding a DSO. **Optimize the name for the Series A buyer, not the design partner. Mom is sold by you, not by the brand.**

Recommended archetype: **Infrastructure Noun** (Stripe, Plaid, Mercury, Rippling) — claims that we're a layer in dental practice operations, not a feature. Survives both a 4-doc owner and a 200-location DSO RCM director.

## Names to reject outright (per search-specialist competitive map)

- Anything containing **AI, Health, Smart, Intelligent, Dental, Endo** as a primary anchor — crowded, undifferentiated, ages instantly.
- **Pearl/Pearly variants** — Pearl AI, Pearl Dental Software, Pearly Billing all in active use. Phonetic + SEO collision certain.
- **Vyne/Vine/Vein variants** — Vyne Dental owns the dental RCM clearinghouse space; Vyne Trellis sits next to it. Direct competitor adjacency.
- **Endo-prefix solo** (EndoFlow, EndoBot, EndoPro) — locks TAM, dates instantly, several already exist or are obvious takes.
- **Acronyms** (PBS, TDO, DI) — already owned by the PMS incumbents and forgettable as a category.

## White space (per search-specialist)

Of the directions identified as truly open in dental SaaS:
1. **Operator/trade vocabulary** — speak the endodontist's own language back to them
2. **Action verb framing** — only Weave occupies this in dental, and it's in patient comms (different layer)
3. **Hospitality / service-industry language** — completely unexploited; uniquely natural fit for Method Co. founder
4. **Place anchors** — completely unexploited
5. **Narrative/story-driven names** — Advocate, Compass, Bridge, etc. — emotional differentiator

## Recommendation

**Lead candidate: LEDGER**

- Two of four agents arrived at it independently — cross-lens convergence is the strongest signal in this exercise.
- Operator-grade word, finance-rooted (matches the actual domain: claims + records).
- Survives the spoken/email/conference/vendor-comparison/business-card tests (sales-automator's framework).
- Scales from 4-doc practice to 200-location DSO without rebrand.
- Method Co. parallel: in hospitality, the ledger is the truth-of-record. Same primitive applies here.
- One required check before locking: trademark + .com. `ledger.com` is held by Ledger SAS (crypto). Likely candidates: `getledger.com`, `ledger.dental`, `useledger.com`, `ledger.health`.

**Backup 1: CONCIERGE**

- Content-marketer's #1 pick, and it threads the needle on the founder's hospitality background — the brand sounds like it could only have been built by someone from your industry, which is itself a moat.
- White space (zero hospitality-flavored brands in dental SaaS).
- Risk: "concierge medicine" is a generic category descriptor in healthcare; need a trademark search to confirm we can own it as a brand in our specific category, and a domain search for `concierge.dental` / `getconcierge.com` / similar.

**Backup 2: MERIDIAN**

- Best abstract option. Clinical-adjacent (anatomy/navigation), infrastructural enough to scale.
- Cooler tone than Ledger or Concierge — needs more positioning copy to land emotionally.
- Likely strong defensibility (not heavily used in dental or healthcare automation).

## Decision matrix Ross can run

For each candidate, before locking:

1. **`.com` check** — `[name].com`, `get[name].com`, `[name].health`, `[name].dental`, `use[name].com`
2. **USPTO trademark search** — Class 9 (software) and Class 42 (SaaS); also Class 44 (medical/dental services) if there's a category overlap
3. **State trademark check** — at least Pennsylvania (Method Co. base), Delaware, Michigan, South Carolina, Maryland (Method Co. operating states) before broader filing
4. **AAE conference grep** — does this name already appear in any AAE Annual Meeting exhibitor list 2023-2026? Confirms no in-category collision.
5. **Voice test** — say out loud: "Hi, this is Ross from [name] — we automate insurance pre-auths for endodontic practices." Does it land in 5 seconds?

## Strategic risk to watch

The strategic-analyst put it bluntly: *the pilot is won by the founder's mother and the product, not the name. Optimize the name for the Series A buyer, not the design partner.* Don't fall for an endo-clever name (Apex, Ferrule, Radix) just because it earns a smile from mom's endodontists in week one.
